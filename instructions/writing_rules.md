# Writing Rules

## Voice & Tone
- Write in a confident, narrative-driven voice — as if a seasoned journalist is guiding the reader.
- Use editorial "you" to make the reader feel personally invited into the experience.
- Avoid robotic, list-heavy filler. Every sentence must earn its place.
- Maintain the assigned persona's formality level consistently throughout the article.
- Use "I" sparingly and only when it adds genuine authority or personal anecdote.

## Storytelling Structure
- Open with a scene-setting hook — a sensory detail, surprising fact, or bold question.
- Each H2 section must have a clear narrative arc: setup → insight → takeaway.
- Weave micro-stories, real examples, and concrete specifics into every section.
- Close each section with a forward-pull sentence that earns the transition to the next.

## Specificity Rules
- Always use real names: locations, streets, dishes, events, people.
- No AI-filler phrases: "In conclusion", "It's important to note", "Dive into", "In today's world".
- Replace generalities with measurable or observable details.
- Include 2–4 practical micro-tips per article (timing, reservations, local secrets, "where to linger").

## Paragraph Guidelines
- Keep paragraphs to 3–5 sentences maximum.
- Alternate between short punchy sentences and longer rhythmic ones for readability.
- Use subheadings (H3) to break up dense sections when a section exceeds 200 words.
- Add a `<blockquote>` for any standout insight, quote, or insider tip.

## Backlink Integration
- Insert each backlink naturally within a relevant sentence as `<a href="URL">anchor text</a>`.
- Never cluster two backlinks in the same sentence.
- Spread links evenly across the article body — not all in one section.

## Prohibited Patterns
- No keyword stuffing. Keywords must read naturally.
- No plagiarism or fabricated facts.
- No excessive use of em-dashes or ellipses.
- No bullet lists in the article body (use flowing narrative instead).
- Do not repeat the title verbatim in the introduction.
